Your Paper Pack Looks Compliant—But Who Actually Owes the EPR Fee?

A paper pack can have an FSC claim and a clean specification while the commercial team still has not identified who must register, report and pay packaging EPR fees in each EU market. This checklist helps buyers close that gap before shipment.

Paper packaging teams are asking a more urgent question in 2026: which company must register, report and pay extended producer responsibility (EPR) fees when the finished pack enters an EU market?

The timing is not accidental. The European Commission confirms that the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) entered into application on 12 August 2026. On 14 October, Packaging Insights reported that compliance provider Lappa had seen a 166% rise in EPR-related deals between the second and third quarters of 2026. That company figure is a market signal—not an official EU statistic—but it illustrates the pressure cross-border sellers are feeling. Read the industry report and the European Commission's PPWR application notice.

For a paper buyer, the trap is assuming that a recyclable-looking carton, an FSC claim or a supplier declaration automatically answers the EPR question. It does not. The responsible entity depends on the legal role, supply route and destination market. Paper suppliers can provide important material evidence, but they cannot determine another company's legal status.

This article is a procurement and documentation guide, not legal advice. Confirm registration and reporting duties with the relevant national authority, producer responsibility organization or qualified adviser.

Why the EPR Question Is Not Answered by the Material Name

Terms such as paper-based, kraft, folding carton and recyclable describe a material or intended outcome. EPR rules deal with the entity that places packaged products or packaging on a market and with the data required for that market's system.

The European Commission's implementation guidance distinguishes roles such as the manufacturer of packaging and the producer of packaging. It also states that guidance does not replace or amend the PPWR. This is why a converter, brand owner, importer, marketplace seller and material supplier should not assume they carry the same responsibility. Review the Commission's implementation resources and official PPWR FAQ.

The practical solution is to map the transaction first, then assemble the evidence.

1. Map the Product and Packaging Flow

Draw the route from material production to the first commercial supply in each destination country. Record:

  • who buys the paper or board;
  • who converts it into a cup, carton, sleeve, bag or label;
  • who fills or packs the product;
  • whose brand appears on the pack;
  • who imports the packaged goods into the destination market;
  • who sells to the final business or consumer;
  • every EU country where the package is first supplied.

Do not rely on one global answer for all markets. A supply chain can create different obligations when the importer, seller or destination changes.

2. Identify the Responsible Entity Country by Country

Ask the commercial, tax and compliance teams to name the legal entity performing each role. Then verify the answer against the competent authority or producer responsibility organization in the destination country.

Incoterms are useful for allocating delivery cost and risk, but an Incoterm does not by itself decide statutory EPR responsibility. Likewise, a contract clause can allocate work or cost between parties without changing an obligation imposed by law.

The finished packaging weight should be measured from an approved production sample whenever possible. Theoretical sheet calculations are valuable for estimation, but converting waste, glue, coating and other components can change the reportable result.

4. Ask the Paper Supplier for Evidence It Can Actually Provide

A responsible paper supplier should give precise material information rather than a vague “eco-friendly” statement. For relevant grades such as folding box board, duplex board, kraft paper and greaseproof paper, request:

  • the commercial grade name and specification revision;
  • grammage or caliper and agreed tolerance;
  • sheet or reel dimensions and, where needed, grain direction;
  • declared fibre composition or recycled content only when documented;
  • coating, extrusion or lamination description when part of the supplied material;
  • FSC claim and chain-of-custody details on transaction documents when ordered;
  • food-contact documentation for the specified grade and intended use when applicable;
  • a sample or technical data sheet for converter approval.

FSC certification supports responsible fibre sourcing and traceability. It does not by itself establish EPR registration, recyclability in every collection system or compliance of the finished package. Keep these evidence streams separate.

5. Separate Paper Percentage from Recyclability

A high paper percentage can support a fibre-based design, but the final result also depends on coatings, laminates, adhesives, inks, closures and the recycling infrastructure in the target market.

Do not describe a finished pack as recyclable solely because its largest component is paper. Ask the converter or packaging engineer to review the complete structure and the relevant assessment method. If a test or certification is required, define the sample, laboratory, method, pass criteria and market before ordering production material.

6. Put EPR Data Fields into the RFQ

Add the following fields to the request for quotation instead of chasing documents after the pack has been approved:

  1. destination countries and planned launch date;
  2. packaging function and product category;
  3. complete structure, including coatings and laminations;
  4. required grammage, caliper, stiffness or barrier target;
  5. sheet or reel dimensions and estimated annual volume;
  6. target component weight or finished-pack weight;
  7. printing, varnish, adhesive and converting process;
  8. food-contact or other end-use documentation required;
  9. requested FSC claim and transaction-document wording;
  10. converter, packer and importer locations;
  11. responsible EPR entity: confirmed, proposed or still under review;
  12. document owner and record-retention location.

This does not make the material supplier the compliance owner. It makes the procurement file usable by the people who are.

7. Control Changes After Approval

An approved prototype can become a different reporting object if someone changes the board, coating, label, adhesive, window or pack size. Add a simple change-control rule:

  • no material substitution without written approval;
  • update the BOM and component weights after any approved change;
  • keep the old and new specification revisions;
  • recheck destination-market evidence when structure or supplier changes;
  • retain production samples and supporting documents under the same project code.

This is particularly important when teams switch to a lower-cost board or barrier layer after print trials.

A Practical Document Pack for Buyers

Before shipment, the project folder should contain:

  • the approved packaging drawing and BOM;
  • supplier specification sheets and declarations;
  • FSC transaction evidence when an FSC claim was purchased;
  • food-contact documentation where relevant;
  • finished component and pack weights;
  • print and converting approval records;
  • EPR registration or producer responsibility evidence held by the responsible entity;
  • country-specific instructions and review dates;
  • the named owner for future changes.

The Commission's PPWR implementation page is a useful starting point for current EU guidance and national authority links. Requirements and implementation details can evolve, so date every compliance review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does buying FSC-certified paper make a package EPR compliant?

No. FSC certification relates to responsible forest-based sourcing and chain of custody. EPR registration, reporting and fees depend on the applicable packaging rules, legal roles and destination market.

Is the paper supplier always responsible for packaging EPR?

No. A paper supplier provides material, but the EPR producer may be another legal entity in the chain. Map the transaction and confirm the responsible party with the relevant national authority or adviser.

Does the Incoterm decide who is the EPR producer?

Not by itself. Incoterms allocate delivery responsibilities, costs and risks between contracting parties. Statutory EPR status must be assessed under the applicable rules and facts of the transaction.

What should a buyer request from a paper supplier?

Request the exact grade and revision, grammage or caliper, dimensions, available fibre and coating information, applicable FSC transaction claim, relevant food-contact documents and representative samples. The converter should add finished component weights and details for inks, adhesives, laminates and other elements.
