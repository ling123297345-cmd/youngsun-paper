Overview

Paper buyers frequently choose between virgin fiber, recycled fiber or a blend. The trade-off is not simple: recycled content can strengthen a sustainability story, but may reduce some mechanical properties, increase variability and require careful contaminant management. Virgin fiber provides more predictable performance and is often preferred for demanding applications.

Questions for Suppliers

  • What percentage of recycled content is claimed — and is it PCR or PIR?
  • Which mechanical properties (tensile, tear, burst, stiffness) are guaranteed?
  • How are contaminants controlled and tested?
  • Is there a detectable odor that could affect food or premium products?
  • Is the recycled content claim supported by FSC Recycled or other verified certification?

Frequently Asked Questions

Does recycled paper perform the same as virgin paper?

Not always. Recycled fiber can be weaker and more variable. However, for many applications — particularly structural boards — recycled performance is entirely adequate and more cost-effective.

Is recycled paper always more sustainable?

It depends. Recycling reduces landfill and demand for virgin fiber, but collection, sorting, de-inking and transport also have environmental impacts. A full lifecycle assessment provides the most complete picture.

Can recycled paper be used for food packaging?

Only in grades specifically manufactured and tested for food contact. Recycled fiber may contain contaminants that are not acceptable for direct food contact in some markets.

What does FSC Recycled certification mean?

The product is made from 100% recycled fiber (post-consumer or post-industrial). The FSC chain-of-custody system verifies the fiber source through the supply chain.
