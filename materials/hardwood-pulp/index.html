Hardwood Pulp: Short Fibers for Smoothness, Formation and Print Quality

Hardwood pulp is the surface-quality fiber: it helps paper look uniform, print cleanly and feel smooth.

Overview

Hardwood pulp is made from broadleaf species such as eucalyptus, birch, aspen and maple. Its fibers are shorter and usually more numerous per gram than softwood fibers. This helps create a more even sheet with a smoother surface, good opacity and clean printing performance.

Key Performance Benefits

Better formation

Short fibers distribute evenly, reducing visible unevenness in the sheet.

Smoother surface

Supports fine print detail, coating holdout and consistent ink transfer.

Higher opacity

Helps reduce show-through in printing and writing papers.

Softness and absorbency potential

Important in many tissue and hygiene furnishes, depending on refining and sheet design.

Production and Fiber Structure

Eucalyptus pulp is widely used for smoothness, softness, optical properties and efficient refining. Birch pulp can provide good bonding, density, internal strength and surface quality. The correct choice depends on the grade and the mill's furnish design.

Applications

  • Woodfree offset and copy paper - Formation, opacity, print uniformity and surface quality Checks: Brightness, opacity, smoothness, stiffness and runnability
  • Coated art paper and board - Uniform base sheet for coating and high-resolution printing Checks: Coat weight, gloss, PPS/Bekk smoothness, ink drying and pick resistance
  • Folding box board surface layers - Smooth, bright printable top surface Checks: Print result, cracking at folds, stiffness and internal bond
  • Tissue and hygiene grades - Softness, absorbency and formation Checks: Softness, tensile, absorbency, dust and wet performance

Questions for Suppliers

  • What are the guaranteed smoothness, opacity, brightness and formation levels?
  • Does the grade use eucalyptus, birch or a mixed hardwood furnish?
  • How is strength reinforced for high-speed printing or difficult converting?
  • Is the surface coated, sized, calendered or embossed after papermaking?
  • Can the supplier provide a print sample or trial sheet from the same grade?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is hardwood pulp lower quality because its fibers are shorter?

No. Short fibers are valuable for formation, smoothness, opacity and softness. They serve a different function from long-fiber reinforcement.

Is eucalyptus pulp only used for tissue?

No. It is also widely used in printing and writing papers, coated grades, paperboard and specialty papers.

Why blend hardwood and softwood pulp?

The blend combines surface quality and opacity from hardwood with strength and runnability from softwood.

Does hardwood pulp guarantee better printing?

It supports a good base sheet, but print quality also depends on coating, sizing, calendering, moisture, ink and press settings.
