Lower Paper Packaging Costs: Which Savings Survive a Trial?

A cheaper tonne is not necessarily a cheaper finished pack. Compare accepted output and validate material changes before committing to a saving.

In This Guide

  1. Establish a baseline
  2. Test the changes that can actually affect cost
  3. Improve sheet yield before assuming a thinner grade is enough
  4. Keep logistics and purchasing assumptions visible
  5. Run a controlled trial
  6. Frequently Asked Questions

Start with cost per accepted finished pack, not paper price alone. A lower material price can be offset by waste, slower converting, additional coatings, damage or rejected output. The following framework is a purchasing method, not a promised savings percentage.

Establish a baseline

Record the current grade, GSM, caliper, sheet format, purchase quantity, print and finishing route, material consumption, accepted output and relevant defects. Keep the performance criteria explicit: appearance, crease quality, glue bond, stiffness or protection as the application requires.

Use the same cost scope for every option. Include material, converting, packing, freight, handling and measurable losses. Avoid counting a cost twice when it is already included in a supplier's quotation.

Improve sheet yield before assuming a thinner grade is enough

Compare the layout against the purchased sheet area, allowing for gripper, trim, grain direction and process constraints. A theoretical nesting gain is not a production saving until the printer or converter approves it.

Reducing GSM changes mass per area; it does not prove equivalent stiffness or finished-pack performance. For folding box board, request the exact grade data and trial the crease and print result. For grey board, check the assembled structure and wrap bond. Do not treat different board families as direct substitutes solely because their GSM matches.

Keep logistics and purchasing assumptions visible

Compare order sizes using actual delivered quotations and realistic stock consumption. A larger shipment may change freight cost per unit while increasing cash tied up in inventory. A supply agreement may improve planning, but a discount, fixed price or priority allocation exists only if agreed in writing.

Use our ocean freight checklist to compare the same delivery scope. Under CIF, the ICC explains that freight payment and transit risk transfer are different points; insurance and claims arrangements belong in the comparison too.

Run a controlled trial

  1. Select one change and define acceptance criteria before production.
  2. Keep artwork, equipment settings and other conditions comparable where practical; record differences.
  3. Measure consumed material, accepted output, defects and additional processing.
  4. Check the assembled pack under the intended handling and use conditions.
  5. Recalculate cost per accepted pack and record the limits of the trial.

Do not turn an unmeasured proposal into a customer success story. Report the actual result, including cases where the original material remains the better choice. Recheck performance when the product, format or production route changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a lower GSM always save money?

No. Any material saving must be compared with yield, processing and the required pack performance.

Is recycled content always the cheaper option?

No. Compare the actual grade, availability and production result rather than assuming price or quality from fibre origin alone.

What should I provide for a cost review?

Share the current specification, format, application, finishing process and problem to solve. Ask our team to review relevant alternatives without assuming a guaranteed saving.
