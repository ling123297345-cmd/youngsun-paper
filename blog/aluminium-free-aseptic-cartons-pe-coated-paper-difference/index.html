Aluminium-Free Aseptic Cartons: What Replaces the Missing Layer?

The latest aluminium-free beverage cartons still rely on an engineered barrier. For buyers, the important distinction is between a coated paper component and a validated aseptic packaging system.

Removing aluminium does not remove the need for a barrier. It also does not make an aseptic beverage carton plastic-free, or turn ordinary PE-coated paper into a substitute for a qualified aseptic packaging material. These are different purchasing decisions.

On 28 October 2026, Packaging Europe reported SIG and LSDH's introduction of aluminium-free aseptic cartons in France, with the first products expected on shelves by the end of the year. The announcement concerns a particular packaging technology and filling operation, not a general approval for any coated board.

The useful lesson for buyers is how to describe what they need to buy: a paper component, a converted laminate, or a complete compatible packaging system. Confusing these levels makes quotations difficult to compare and can leave important responsibilities undefined.

The missing aluminium layer has a replacement function

SIG's published technical description of its material explains that a polymer-based barrier replaces the aluminium layer. That is a manufacturer-specific construction, not proof that every polymer coating delivers the same protection.

Ask the packaging system supplier which barrier functions are required for your beverage and how the proposed structure delivers them. Liquid containment, oxygen protection, light protection, aroma retention and seal performance should not be treated as interchangeable claims. Do not infer these properties simply because a sheet looks glossy or repels a drop of water.

For procurement, request the material designation and revision, intended application and relevant performance evidence. If a layer changes, ask which earlier approvals and trials still apply. A description such as "paper plus polymer" is too broad to define an equivalent supply.

Why PE-coated paper is not an aseptic specification

PE-coated paper describes a material category. Cup paper describes a different familiar application. Neither name, by itself, establishes suitability for long-life aseptic beverages.

An aseptic pack depends on the selected material, its converting and sealing behaviour, the closure where used, and a compatible controlled filling process. The finished product and intended storage conditions matter too. Changing only the paper supplier is not the same decision as qualifying the complete beverage pack.

Imagine two enquiries: one asks for paper to convert into a takeaway cup; the other asks for a carton for a juice sold unrefrigerated before opening. The paper surfaces might appear similar in a sample photograph. The technical responsibilities and evidence needed are not. This is an illustrative distinction, not a YOUNGSUN performance test or a recommendation to use one material for both.

The lowest price per tonne is not a meaningful comparison if one offer includes only a paper component and another includes a qualified system material. Identify exclusions explicitly: printing, closures, sealing trials, filling support, testing and responsibility for finished-pack acceptance.

Request a conversion and filling trial plan

Before committing to a change, agree who owns each decision. The material producer, converter, filling equipment supplier, co-packer and brand quality team may have different responsibilities. A supplier's statement that its own system works does not establish compatibility with an unrelated machine or process.

Ask which material format and dimensions the line accepts, which settings require review, how seals and closures will be checked, and what happens to trial output. Define acceptance criteria and the evidence needed before commercial release. Do not promise a line speed, an unchanged reject rate or a drop-in conversion without a documented basis.

For a buyer working with a co-packer in Latin America, start with that co-packer's accepted packaging specifications. Importing a material before confirming local filling capability can leave you with stock that cannot be used as intended.

Keep shelf-life claims product-specific

Do not copy a shelf-life number from an industry launch into your own specification. Ask the responsible packaging and food teams for evidence covering the actual beverage, pack format, processing and storage conditions. A claim for another recipe or filling arrangement does not settle your case.

Chemical food-contact suitability and shelf-life validation are also separate questions. The European Commission's food-contact overview explains the safety principles governing materials used with food in the EU. It is not an approval of a particular carton, and it does not establish rules for every Latin American destination. Confirm applicable local requirements with the responsible importer and qualified technical advisers.

Check the scope of environmental claims

"No aluminium layer" describes one feature. It does not establish plastic-free composition, universal recyclability or a carbon saving for your own pack. The cap, remaining coatings and the actual collection and recycling route still need consideration.

If a quotation includes an emissions reduction, request the underlying comparison. SIG's life-cycle assessment summary illustrates why the package version, comparison basis and market context matter. Do not reuse another manufacturer's percentage for a different construction, factory or country.

Send an enquiry that avoids the wrong quotation

State the beverage, refrigerated or ambient use, target market, intended format, filling partner and the exact component being purchased. Attach the converter's or filler's approved specification when available. If you are still selecting a system, say so rather than asking a paper mill to assume the missing requirements.

Frequently asked questions

Does aluminium-free mean plastic-free?

No. The described SIG technology replaces the aluminium layer with a polymer-based barrier. Check the exact construction rather than interpreting aluminium-free as the absence of all plastics.

Can PE-coated cup paper replace aseptic carton material?

Not simply because both use paper and a coating. Require evidence for the intended beverage, packaging structure and filling system before considering any substitution.

Is a food-contact report enough to promise a shelf life?

No. Food-contact suitability does not by itself validate the filled product's shelf life. The responsible technical teams must evaluate the complete product and process.

What should a paper-component enquiry include?

Specify the component, application, contact conditions, destination, converting process, dimensions and approved technical requirements. Make clear which downstream operations and validations are outside the paper supply.
