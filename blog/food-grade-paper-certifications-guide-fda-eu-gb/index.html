Food-Contact Paper Documents: What Buyers Must Verify

A test report, a supplier declaration and an FSC certificate answer different questions. This guide explains how to match each document to the supplied paper and intended food-contact use.

Food-contact suitability belongs to a specified material used under specified conditions. A paper name, laboratory logo or phrase such as “FDA certified” is not enough to establish it.

Begin with the grade, full construction, food type, contact side, time, temperature and destination country. Then ask what each document actually demonstrates. This is a buyer's documentation checklist, not a legal opinion or a certificate for YOUNGSUN products. Regulatory references were reviewed on 24 October 2026; confirm the currently applicable requirements for the order.

Match the document to the question

DocumentWhat it can help establishWhat it does not establish by itself
Technical data sheetGrade identity and declared physical propertiesComplete food-contact compliance
Supplier compliance statementResponsibility, legal references and stated use scopeEvery use outside that scope
Laboratory reportResults for the identified sample, methods and conditionsAutomatic approval of other grades, formulas or uses
FSC certificate and transaction claimApplicable forest-sourcing chain-of-custody informationFood-contact safety, grease resistance or heat suitability

United States: check the authorization route and use scope

The FDA's food-contact substance inventory identifies regulatory routes and also points to separate notification and exemption inventories. Relevant paper provisions can include 21 CFR 176.170 and 176.180, but selecting a section number is not a complete assessment of a coated or printed construction.

Request the supplier's basis for the exact composition and use. Where a food-contact notification is relied on, confirm its applicability rather than treating a notification number as general approval for every manufacturer or material.

The FDA's food types and conditions of use distinguish the food and exposure conditions. A document for dry ambient use should not be assumed to cover fatty food, hot fill or baking.

European Union: framework, specific measures and national requirements

The European Commission's legislation overview explains the general framework and specific measures. For paper-based packaging, review the complete construction, applicable good-manufacturing requirements and relevant national rules. A plastic coating can require additional assessment.

Do not assume one identical declaration format is mandatory for every material throughout the EU. Written-declaration duties depend on applicable specific measures and national provisions. Ask the responsible supplier or compliance adviser which requirements apply in the destination country and request supporting evidence accordingly.

China: identify the applicable standard and edition

GB 4806.8 concerns food-contact paper and board; GB 9685 concerns the use of additives. Neither should be treated as a standalone universal “food-grade certificate.” The Ganzhou market regulator's explanation of GB 4806.8-2022 records that edition's implementation from 30 June 2023.

For a new order, have the supplier or qualified laboratory identify the current applicable editions and any additional requirements for coatings, adhesives, inks or the finished article. A paper-base report alone may not cover the supplied construction.

PFAS statements need a defined meaning

Ask whether the statement concerns intentionally added PFAS, a specified list of substances, a screening measure or another defined scope. A non-detect result means below the stated analytical limit for the analytes and sample tested, not proof of absolute absence of every PFAS.

There is no universal 10 ppb threshold that can be copied into every paper RFQ. Record the legal or customer requirement, method, analytes, units and detection/reporting limits. Do not treat grease resistance, FSC status or a coating name as evidence of PFAS absence.

Review reports by change and relevance, not an invented expiry rule

A two-year-old report is not automatically invalid, and a recent report is not automatically relevant. Check product code, manufacturing source, sample construction, test conditions and whether formulation or production has changed. Follow any actual legal, certification or customer validity requirement.

If the paper grade, coating, ink, adhesive or use condition changes, ask whether the existing assessment still covers the supplied product. Keep the response with the order approval record.

Build a six-part approval file

  1. Exact grade, supplier/manufacturer identity and sample reference.
  2. Complete construction, including relevant coating, print and adhesive components.
  3. Intended food, contact side, duration, temperature and destination.
  4. Applicable legal basis and scoped supplier statement.
  5. Relevant test reports, method details and traceable sample identification.
  6. Approved sample, revision date and a written change-notification rule.

Ask our paper team about available documentation, specifying the exact grade and use. Compare cup paper, greaseproof paper and PE-coated paper by their required function. Availability and scope of documents must be checked for the quoted material; this guide makes no blanket promise of SGS, PFAS or food-contact certification across the range.

Frequently asked questions

Does FSC certification demonstrate food-contact safety?

No. Forest-sourcing and food-contact assessments have different scopes. Request the relevant evidence separately.

Does every laboratory report expire after two years?

No universal two-year rule applies to all food-contact paper reports. Check the actual requirement and whether the sample, construction and conditions remain representative.

Can one document cover every GSM and coating?

Only if its stated scope and supporting assessment cover those variants. Ask the responsible supplier to identify the covered range and limits.

What should I do when a PFAS report says not detected?

Read the analytes, method, units and reporting limits. Compare that scope with the destination and customer requirement; do not interpret it as absolute absence of all PFAS.
